Spicy: Open Source Restaurant Management System

Spicy is a restaurant management system. It handles the counter, the kitchen, the bar, your stock, your shifts, and your accounts, on one computer inside your restaurant.

It runs on your own network. Nothing is sent to an outside service, and it keeps working when the internet is down. That means your sales figures, supplier details, and staff records stay on hardware you control.

Spicy suits a single restaurant. One location, one set of settings, one database.

What Spicy does

  • Point of sale. Cashiers take orders and payments at the counter. Food prints to the kitchen, drinks print to the bar.
  • Stock. Drinks are deducted automatically when they are sold. Food ingredients are counted by the kitchen at the end of the day.
  • Shifts and cash. Open with a float, count the drawer at the end, and the system shows what it expected against what you counted.
  • Accounting. Every sale writes balanced entries to a proper ledger, with food and drinks reported separately.
  • Daily profit and loss. See your gross sales, what the food and drinks cost you, your expenses, and what you actually made.
  • Suppliers. Record what you received, what you were billed, and what you still owe.
  • Reports. Sales by day, item, staff member, service type, and hour. Ledger, trial balance, and stock reports.

Who uses Spicy

Spicy has three roles, and each person sees only what they need.

  • Cashier takes orders, sends them to the kitchen, takes payment, and closes their shift. They see the POS and nothing else.
  • Manager runs the back office: menu, stock, shifts, orders, accounts, and reports. They also cancel orders and process refunds.
  • Administrator does everything a manager does, plus creating accounts and assigning roles.

Setup covers creating accounts and assigning roles. Back office covers what a manager can reach.

How Spicy works day to day

A normal day runs like this:

  1. The cashier opens a shift and counts the float into the drawer.
  2. Customers order. The cashier builds the order, sends it, and takes payment.
  3. Food prints to the kitchen. Drinks print to the bar.
  4. At closing time the cashier counts the drawer and compares it to what the system expected.
  5. The kitchen counts how much rice, oil, and chicken it actually used.
  6. The manager produces that day's profit and loss.

Steps 5 and 6 matter more than they look. Drink costs are known exactly because Spicy watched them sell. Food costs are only as accurate as the kitchen's count, which is why Spicy warns you when a count is missing rather than showing a flattering zero.

Getting started

Two steps, in this order:

  1. Install Spicy — get it running on the cashier's computer.
  2. Set up your restaurant — add your items, prices, payment methods, and stock.

New here? Work through Installation and then Setup in order. Setup assumes Spicy is already running.
