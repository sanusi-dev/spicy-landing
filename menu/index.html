Menu

Adding dishes and drinks, setting prices, and taking things off sale without losing your work.

New to Spicy? The one-time menu setup is in Setup. This page is about changing things afterwards.

Menus

Go to Menu → Menus. A menu is the list of items your cashier sees, with their prices.

You can have more than one — a main menu, a lunch list, a drinks-only list for the bar. Set which one is live under Settings → Restaurant settings, on Active menu. The POS only ever shows the active one.

Turning a menu off hides everything on it from the POS without deleting any prices. Useful for a seasonal menu.

Menu items and prices

Go to Menu → Menu items. Each row links a dish or drink to your menu at a price.

  • Rate — what the customer pays. Leave it blank and Spicy fills it from what you last paid for the item, as a starting point.
  • Special dish — puts the item under a quick filter on the POS for featured dishes.
  • Disabled — takes the item off the POS right away, keeping its price in the database.

Use Disabled rather than deleting. Running out of a dish or a drink is normal. Disable it, sell it again tomorrow, and you have not lost your price or your history.

A price change affects new orders only. Orders already taken keep the price they were rung up at, so yesterday's sales figures never change retroactively.

Sizes

For a dish sold in sizes — quarter, half, full chicken:

  1. Create each size as its own item under Inventory → Items. Tick Sell this item and leave stock tracking off, exactly as you would for a dish.
  2. On the parent dish, tick Use variants. The parent becomes a template and stops being sellable itself.
  3. Go to Menu → Variants and link the parent to each size.
  4. Add each size to your menu at its own price.

On the POS, the parent shows as one tile with a price range — "₦1,200 – ₦3,500". Your cashier taps it and picks the size. The size joins the order as its own line at its own price.

Add-ons

For extras like "extra cheese" or "extra shot":

  1. Create the extra as its own item, sellable, priced.
  2. Add it to your menu at its price.
  3. Go to Menu → Add-ons and link it to the dish it belongs to.

When the cashier picks it, the extra appears on the bill as its own line at its own price. Jollof rice at ₦2,000 plus extra plantain at ₦500 shows as two lines totalling ₦2,500.

Add-ons and sizes are separate items with separate prices. They are not "modifiers" that adjust the parent's price. An add-on always adds its full price on top.

Changing prices

Prices are edited on the menu item, not on the inventory item — the same dish can cost different amounts on different menus.

To change prices across a whole menu at once, there is no bulk edit. Work down the list of menu items. If you have many prices to change, a quick pattern is to open the item, change the rate, and work through the list.

Removing something properly

  • Temporarily unavailable — tick Disabled on the menu item. Back on sale next week with the price intact.
  • Permanly off the menu — delete the menu item. The underlying item stays in the system, so your sales history is untouched.
  • Never delete the underlying item if it has been sold. It holds your history and any stock count.

Item images

Add a photo on the item and it shows on the POS tile, making it faster for your cashier to find things. Without one, Spicy shows the item's initials instead.

Photos are optional. For a large menu, prioritise the items you take most orders for.

Check your prices against cost

Every dish with a recipe shows a plate cost — what one portion should cost at current prices. Check it against your selling price.

A dish selling well below its plate cost is working you every time you sell it. This is often invisible until you look.
